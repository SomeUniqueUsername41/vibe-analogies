Nintendo, Sega, and Namco Games

Comparing games by the company they belong to and the design philosophy, arcade energy, or general personality of another major Japanese game company.

Is a Nintendo game and feels like a Nintendo game

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time

Its colorful fantasy world, inventive mechanics, memorable characters, sense of discovery, and carefully polished adventure design make it one of the clearest examples of Nintendo's classic game-design philosophy.

Nintendo → Nintendo

Is a Nintendo game but feels like a Sega game

F-Zero GX

Its extreme speed, arcade attitude, futuristic spectacle, and aggressive difficulty feel very Sega-like. Appropriately, the game was also developed with Sega's arcade expertise.

Nintendo → Sega

Is a Nintendo game but feels like a Namco game

Star Fox: Assault

Its arcade shooting, science-fiction combat, mission structure, and heavier emphasis on action make it feel at home beside Namco's arcade and sci-fi action games.

Nintendo → Namco

Is a Sega game but feels like a Nintendo game

Billy Hatcher and the Giant Egg

Colorful worlds, a cheerful mascot hero, playful mechanics, and a whimsical platforming adventure give it the feel of an experimental Nintendo title.

Sega → Nintendo

Is a Sega game and feels like a Sega game

Sonic Adventure 2

Speed, attitude, rock music, arcade-style scoring, ambitious spectacle, and a distinctly turn-of-the- millennium cool factor make it quintessential Sega.

Sega → Sega

Is a Sega game but feels like a Namco game

The House of the Dead 2

Although unmistakably Sega, its intense arcade-shooter structure and light-gun action make it feel closely related to the sort of arcade experience associated with Namco's Time Crisis.

Sega → Namco

Is a Namco game but feels like a Nintendo game

Klonoa: Door to Phantomile

A lovable mascot, colorful fantasy environments, clever platforming mechanics, and emotional storybook presentation make Klonoa feel unusually compatible with Nintendo's platforming tradition.

Namco → Nintendo

Is a Namco game but feels like a Sega game

Time Crisis II

Its fast-paced arcade presentation, light-gun action, dramatic spectacle, and emphasis on reflexes fit comfortably beside Sega arcade games such as Virtua Cop and The House of the Dead.

Namco → Sega

Is a Namco game and feels like a Namco game

Tekken 3

Polished 3D arcade combat, eccentric fighters, technical gameplay, dramatic presentation, and unmistakable late-1990s arcade energy make it one of the clearest examples of classic Namco.

Namco → Namco

Nintendo, Sega, and Namco Characters

The same three-way comparison, but applied to character design, personality, genre, and mascot energy.

Is a Nintendo character and feels like a Nintendo character

Mario

Friendly, instantly readable, colorful, versatile, and built around simple but expressive movement, Mario is essentially Nintendo's design philosophy personified.

Nintendo → Nintendo

Is a Nintendo character but feels like a Sega character

Captain Falcon

His exaggerated confidence, futuristic racing background, flashy moves, speed, and larger-than- life personality give him the sort of arcade coolness strongly associated with Sega.

Nintendo → Sega

Is a Nintendo character but feels like a Namco character

Fox McCloud

As the heroic pilot of an arcade-style science-fiction combat series, Fox fits naturally beside the pilots and futuristic action heroes found throughout Namco's arcade history.

Nintendo → Namco

Is a Sega character but feels like a Nintendo character

Billy Hatcher

His cheerful design, simple heroic personality, colorful fantasy world, and unusual gameplay gimmick make him feel like the protagonist of a Nintendo platforming experiment.

Sega → Nintendo

Is a Sega character and feels like a Sega character

Sonic the Hedgehog

Speed, attitude, sneakers, bright colors, arcade energy, and deliberate coolness make Sonic the clearest possible expression of Sega's mascot identity.

Sega → Sega

Is a Sega character but feels like a Namco character

Akira Yuki

As a serious martial artist from a technical 3D fighting series, Akira could fit remarkably naturally into the roster of a Namco fighter such as Tekken.

Sega → Namco

Is a Namco character but feels like a Nintendo character

Klonoa

Cute, expressive, heroic, easy to recognize, and built around a colorful platforming world, Klonoa has the qualities of a classic Nintendo mascot despite belonging to Namco.

Namco → Nintendo

Is a Namco character but feels like a Sega character

Rick Taylor

The masked Splatterhouse protagonist has the aggressive, edgy, arcade-brawler personality that fits comfortably with the harder-edged side of Sega's character library.

Namco → Sega

Is a Namco character and feels like a Namco character

Pac-Man

Simple shapes, instantly understandable gameplay, arcade history, universal appeal, and one of the most recognizable designs in video games make Pac-Man quintessential Namco.

Namco → Namco

Pokémon and Digimon

Comparing two monster franchises by creature design, personality, evolutionary style, and overall aesthetic.

Is a Pokémon and feels like a Pokémon

Pikachu

Cute, animal-inspired, approachable, expressive, and built around one clear elemental ability, Pikachu represents the classic Pokémon design philosophy almost perfectly.

Pokémon → Pokémon

Is a Pokémon but feels like a Digimon

Lucario

Lucario's humanoid proportions, martial-arts abilities, aura powers, serious personality, and anime-hero quality make it resemble the more character-driven designs common in Digimon.

Pokémon → Digimon

Is a Digimon but feels like a Pokémon

Patamon

Small, cute, animal-like, immediately readable, and relatively simple in design, Patamon could easily pass for a creature encountered in a Pokémon region.

Digimon → Pokémon

Is a Digimon and feels like a Digimon

Agumon

A talking dinosaur partner who can transform into increasingly elaborate and powerful forms, Agumon embodies Digimon's mixture of friendship, combat, evolution, and anime adventure.

Digimon → Digimon

Autobots and Decepticons

Comparing Transformers characters by faction and by whether their personality, morality, or combat style resembles the opposite side.

Is an Autobot and feels like an Autobot

Optimus Prime

Noble, protective, self-sacrificing, diplomatic, and willing to fight when necessary, Optimus is the defining expression of the Autobot ideal.

Autobot → Autobot

Is an Autobot but feels like a Decepticon

Grimlock

Brutal strength, aggression, pride, a monstrous alternate form, and frequent resistance to authority give Grimlock much more of a Decepticon-like edge than the typical Autobot.

Autobot → Decepticon

Is a Decepticon but feels like an Autobot

Thundercracker

In portrayals that emphasize his doubts about the Decepticon cause, Thundercracker can seem more reflective and morally conflicted than his fellow Seekers, giving him an unusually Autobot-like side.

Decepticon → Autobot

Is a Decepticon and feels like a Decepticon

Megatron

Ruthless ambition, overwhelming firepower, authoritarian leadership, and an obsession with conquest make Megatron the archetypal Decepticon.

Decepticon → Decepticon

Maximals and Predacons

Is a Maximal and feels like a Maximal

Optimus Primal

Responsible, compassionate, courageous, and protective of his crew, Optimus Primal represents the cooperative and heroic ideals of the Maximals.

Maximal → Maximal

Is a Maximal but feels like a Predacon

Dinobot

Proud, intimidating, confrontational, obsessed with strength and honor, and originally a Predacon himself, Dinobot retains much of the opposing faction's personality even after joining the Maximals.

Maximal → Predacon

Is a Predacon but feels like a Maximal

Blackarachnia

Her independence, increasing moral complexity, emotional connections, and eventual movement toward the Maximals make her feel less naturally Predacon than her original allegiance suggests.

Predacon → Maximal

Is a Predacon and feels like a Predacon

Megatron

Scheming, theatrical, manipulative, ambitious, and determined to dominate both his enemies and his own troops, Beast Wars Megatron is Predacon energy distilled into one character.

Predacon → Predacon

Killer Instinct and Virtua Fighter

Comparing the exaggerated monsters, cyborgs, supernatural fighters, and arcade spectacle of Killer Instinct with the more grounded martial-arts approach of Virtua Fighter.

Is a Killer Instinct character and feels like a Killer Instinct character

Fulgore

A heavily armed cybernetic warrior with an intimidating design, energy attacks, blades, lasers, and extreme arcade spectacle, Fulgore represents Killer Instinct's over-the-top fighting-game identity almost perfectly.

Killer Instinct → Killer Instinct

Is a Killer Instinct character but feels like a Virtua Fighter character

T.J. Combo

Compared with Killer Instinct's monsters, supernatural warriors, and cybernetic fighters, T.J. Combo is a relatively grounded human athlete whose combat style centers on recognizable boxing techniques and physical skill, making him unusually compatible with Virtua Fighter's martial-arts- focused roster.

Killer Instinct → Virtua Fighter

Is a Virtua Fighter character but feels like a Killer Instinct character

Dural

Dural's metallic body, mysterious origins, superhuman presence, and ability to imitate the techniques of other fighters make her far stranger and more fantastical than Virtua Fighter's mostly human roster. She could fit naturally among Killer Instinct's cyborgs, mutants, and supernatural combatants.

Virtua Fighter → Killer Instinct

Is a Virtua Fighter character and feels like a Virtua Fighter character

Akira Yuki

Serious, disciplined, technically demanding, and focused on a recognizable traditional martial art, Akira embodies Virtua Fighter's emphasis on grounded combat, precise technique, and mastery over spectacle.

Virtua Fighter → Virtua Fighter

Barsoom and Alien Legion

Romantic sword-and-planet adventure versus gritty, multicultural interstellar military science fiction.

Is a Barsoom character and feels like a Barsoom character

Tars Tarkas

A gigantic four-armed Green Martian warrior defined by personal honor, tribal culture, physical combat, and unlikely friendship, Tars Tarkas embodies Barsoom's alien sword-and-planet atmosphere.

Barsoom → Barsoom

Is a Barsoom character but feels like an Alien Legion character

Tan Hadron

As a young Red Martian naval officer, Tan Hadron has more of a professional military identity than many of Barsoom's wandering princes, warriors, and adventurers.

Barsoom → Alien Legion

Is an Alien Legion character but feels like a Barsoom character

Jugger Grimrod

Grimrod's imposing alien physique, aggressive personality, and identity as a formidable warrior give him the aura of one of Barsoom's strange and dangerous fighting men.

Alien Legion → Barsoom

Is an Alien Legion character and feels like an Alien Legion character

Sarigar

A visibly alien professional military commander leading soldiers of many different species, Sarigar perfectly represents Alien Legion's "Foreign Legion in space" identity.

Alien Legion → Alien Legion

Nickelodeon and Cartoon Network

Comparing cartoons by the network that actually aired them and the network whose creative personality they resemble.

Is a Nickelodeon show and feels like a Nickelodeon show

SpongeBob SquarePants

Bright colors, eccentric characters, slapstick, absurd humor, memorable catchphrases, and kid-friendly weirdness make SpongeBob almost synonymous with Nickelodeon's cartoon identity.

Nickelodeon → Nickelodeon

Is a Nickelodeon show but feels like a Cartoon Network show

My Life as a Teenage Robot

Its geometric visual style, retro-futuristic setting, superhero action, and highly stylized animation make it resemble the creator-driven action-comedies associated with Cartoon Network.

Nickelodeon → Cartoon Network

Is a Cartoon Network show but feels like a Nickelodeon show

Camp Lazlo

Its loose character comedy, oddball cast, expressive voices, and the creative influence of Rocko's Modern Life creator Joe Murray give it a particularly Nickelodeon-like personality.

Cartoon Network → Nickelodeon

Is a Cartoon Network show and feels like a Cartoon Network show

Dexter's Laboratory

Bold graphic design, exaggerated action, strange science-fiction concepts, and creator-driven comedy make Dexter's Laboratory one of the clearest examples of classic Cartoon Network style.

Cartoon Network → Cartoon Network
