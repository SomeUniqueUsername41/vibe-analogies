Things That Feel Like...

Games, characters, cartoons, and fictional worlds sorted not only by what franchise or company they actually belong to, but by what other franchise, company, genre, or creative style they feel like.

Nintendo, Sega, and Namco Games

Is a Nintendo game and feels like a Nintendo game:
The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time

Its colorful fantasy world, inventive mechanics, memorable characters, sense of discovery, and carefully polished adventure design make it one of the clearest examples of Nintendo's classic game-design philosophy.

Is a Nintendo game but feels like a Sega game:
F-Zero GX

Its extreme speed, arcade attitude, futuristic spectacle, and aggressive difficulty give it a distinctly Sega-like personality. Appropriately, the game was also developed with Sega's arcade expertise.

Is a Nintendo game but feels like a Namco game:
Star Fox: Assault

Its arcade shooting, science-fiction combat, mission structure, and action-heavy presentation make it feel at home beside Namco's arcade and science-fiction games.

Is a Sega game but feels like a Nintendo game:
Billy Hatcher and the Giant Egg

Colorful worlds, a cheerful mascot hero, playful mechanics, and whimsical platforming make it feel like an experimental Nintendo title.

Is a Sega game and feels like a Sega game:
Sonic Adventure 2

Speed, attitude, rock music, arcade-style scoring, and ambitious spectacle make it quintessential Sega.

Is a Sega game but feels like a Namco game:
The House of the Dead 2

Its intense arcade-shooter structure and light-gun action resemble the sort of arcade experience associated with Namco games such as Time Crisis.

Is a Namco game but feels like a Nintendo game:
Klonoa: Door to Phantomile

A lovable mascot, colorful fantasy environments, clever platforming, and storybook presentation give Klonoa a Nintendo-like quality.

Is a Namco game but feels like a Sega game:
Time Crisis II

Its fast-paced arcade presentation, light-gun action, dramatic spectacle, and emphasis on reflexes fit comfortably beside Sega arcade games.

Is a Namco game and feels like a Namco game:
Tekken 3

Polished 3D arcade combat, eccentric fighters, technical gameplay, and 1990s arcade energy make it quintessential Namco.

Nintendo, Sega, and Namco Characters

Is a Nintendo character and feels like a Nintendo character:
Mario

Friendly, colorful, versatile, instantly recognizable, and built around simple but expressive movement, Mario embodies Nintendo's character design.

Is a Nintendo character but feels like a Sega character:
Captain Falcon

His futuristic racing background, speed, confidence, flashy attacks, and larger-than-life personality give him Sega-like arcade coolness.

Is a Nintendo character but feels like a Namco character:
Fox McCloud

His role as a heroic pilot in an arcade-style science-fiction combat series makes him feel compatible with Namco's futuristic action characters.

Is a Sega character but feels like a Nintendo character:
Billy Hatcher

His cheerful design, colorful fantasy world, simple heroism, and unusual gameplay gimmick resemble a Nintendo platforming protagonist.

Is a Sega character and feels like a Sega character:
Sonic the Hedgehog

Speed, attitude, sneakers, bright colors, and arcade energy make Sonic the clearest expression of Sega's mascot identity.

Is a Sega character but feels like a Namco character:
Akira Yuki

As a serious martial artist from a technical 3D fighting game, Akira could fit naturally into a Namco fighting series such as Tekken.

Is a Namco character but feels like a Nintendo character:
Klonoa

Cute, expressive, heroic, and associated with colorful platforming worlds, Klonoa has many qualities of a classic Nintendo mascot.

Is a Namco character but feels like a Sega character:
Rick Taylor

His aggressive, edgy, arcade-brawler personality fits comfortably with the harder-edged side of Sega's character library.

Is a Namco character and feels like a Namco character:
Pac-Man

Simple shapes, arcade history, universal appeal, and an instantly recognizable design make Pac-Man quintessential Namco.

Melmacians and Orkans

Is a Melmacian but feels like an Orkan:

Is an Orkan but feels like a Melmacian:
Mork

Mork is unusually emotional, humorous, energetic, and chaotic for an Orkan, giving him much more of the alien-sitcom-troublemaker personality associated with Melmacians such as ALF.

Is an Orkan and feels like an Orkan:
Orson

Emotionally restrained, authoritative, baffled by human behavior, and deeply rooted in Orkan customs, Orson is one of the clearest examples of the stereotypical Orkan personality.

Ben 10 Alien Eras

Comparing Ben's alien transformations by the series in which they were introduced and the series whose alien design philosophy they most resemble.

A Ben 10 alien from the original Ben 10 series that feels like an alien from the original Ben 10 series:
Heatblast

A simple elemental concept, monstrous silhouette, instantly understandable powers, and bold design make Heatblast quintessential original-series Ben 10.

A Ben 10 alien from the original Ben 10 series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Alien Force series:
Way Big

Tall, sleek, humanoid, cosmic, and enormously powerful, Way Big has the cleaner superheroic quality associated with Alien Force.

A Ben 10 alien from the original Ben 10 series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Ultimate Alien series:
Frankenstrike

Large, intimidating, electrically powered, physically strong, and somewhat industrial-looking, Frankenstrike has a heavier Ultimate Alien quality.

A Ben 10 alien from the original Ben 10 series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Omniverse series:
Ditto

His cartoonish proportions, expressive personality, duplication ability, and comedic potential fit naturally with Omniverse.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Alien Force series that feels like an alien from the original Ben 10 series:
Spidermonkey

His animal-inspired design and straightforward combination of agility, strength, and web abilities resemble the original series' creature-based aliens.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Alien Force series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Alien Force series:
Swampfire

Humanoid yet clearly alien, streamlined, versatile, and equipped with several complementary abilities, Swampfire perfectly captures Alien Force.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Alien Force series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Ultimate Alien series:
Lodestar

His metallic body, unusual head structure, and specialized magnetic abilities give him a mechanical, combat-oriented Ultimate Alien quality.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Alien Force series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Omniverse series:
Goop

A living mass of slime controlled by an anti-gravity projector is exactly the kind of bizarre concept that fits Omniverse.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Ultimate Alien series that feels like an alien from the original Ben 10 series:
Fasttrack

His animal-like appearance and immediately understandable super-speed ability resemble the straightforward concepts of the original series.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Ultimate Alien series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Alien Force series:
AmpFibian

Sleek, elegant, mysterious, and equipped with several energy-based abilities, AmpFibian strongly resembles Alien Force's design language.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Ultimate Alien series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Ultimate Alien series:
NRG

A dangerous radioactive energy being sealed inside massive protective armor is specialized, powerful, intimidating, and quintessential Ultimate Alien.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Ultimate Alien series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Omniverse series:
Juryrigg

A manic gremlin-like alien who dismantles and rebuilds machinery at absurd speed has the exaggerated personality and strange niche ability associated with Omniverse.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Omniverse series that feels like an alien from the original Ben 10 series:
Crashhopper

His insectoid appearance and immediately understandable jumping ability make him feel like the kind of transformation Ben could have discovered in the original series.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Omniverse series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Alien Force series:
Gravattack

Powerful, composed, humanoid, and centered around a major science-fiction ability, Gravattack fits Alien Force's more serious style.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Omniverse series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Ultimate Alien series:
Atomix

Extremely powerful, armored-looking, energy-based, and deliberately bombastic, Atomix fits Ultimate Alien's escalating power levels.

A Ben 10 alien from the Ben 10: Omniverse series that feels like an alien from the Ben 10: Omniverse series:
Bloxx

A brightly colored living building-block gorilla with highly flexible construction abilities is bizarre, playful, visually exaggerated, and quintessentially Omniverse.

Image Comics and Dark Horse Comics

Is an Image Comics character and feels like an Image Comics character:
Spawn

A visually extreme supernatural antihero with a dark costume, violent action, creator-owned mythology, and strong 1990s comic-book energy, Spawn is one of the clearest examples of the classic Image Comics identity.

Is an Image Comics character but feels like a Dark Horse Comics character:
The Darkness

Although published through Top Cow/Image, Jackie Estacado's occult powers, demonic mythology, horror elements, morally complicated personality, and mature supernatural stories give The Darkness a strong Dark Horse Comics feel.

Is a Dark Horse Comics character but feels like an Image Comics character:
X

A brutal masked antihero with regenerative abilities and an extremely violent approach to fighting crime, X has the exaggerated, hard-edged superhero energy strongly associated with 1990s Image Comics.

Is a Dark Horse Comics character and feels like a Dark Horse Comics character:
Hellboy

Occult investigations, monsters, folklore, horror, pulp adventure, deadpan humor, and a highly distinctive creator-driven style make Hellboy one of the clearest examples of the Dark Horse Comics identity.

The Tick and Freakazoid!

Is a The Tick character and feels like a The Tick character:
The Tick

Bombastic, absurdly heroic, endlessly confident, and completely sincere about ridiculous superhero situations, The Tick defines the show's parody style.

Is a The Tick character but feels like a Freakazoid! character:
Chairface Chippendale

His bizarre visual gimmick, ridiculous villainy, and cartoonishly elaborate schemes fit naturally with Freakazoid!'s surreal and unpredictable comedy.

Is a Freakazoid! character but feels like a The Tick character:
Lord Bravery

A pompous and somewhat ineffective superhero parody whose humor comes from treating absurd superhero conventions seriously, Lord Bravery feels like he could easily exist in The Tick's world.

Is a Freakazoid! character and feels like a Freakazoid! character:
Freakazoid

Hyperactive, self-aware, unpredictable, fourth-wall-breaking, and driven by pure cartoon logic, Freakazoid is the clearest expression of his show's style.

Bugs Bunny

Clever, theatrical, self-assured, wisecracking, and constantly turning conflicts into elaborate comedy routines, Bugs Bunny is one of the clearest expressions of the classic Looney Tunes identity.

Dragon Ball Humans and Androids

Is a human and feels like a human:
Krillin

Fully human, expressive, humorous, courageous despite obvious limits, and grounded compared with the series' more exotic fighters, Krillin represents the classic Dragon Ball human martial artist.

Is a human but feels like an android:
Tien Shinhan

Extremely disciplined, stoic, precise, emotionally restrained, and relentlessly focused on training, Tien can feel almost machine-like compared with many of Dragon Ball's other human characters.

Is an android but feels like a human:
Android 18

Despite her designation, Android 18 behaves much more like an ordinary person than a stereotypical machine. She is sarcastic, independent, affectionate toward her family, and concerned with everyday human matters.

Android 16

Large, mechanically constructed, initially mission-focused, equipped with built-in weapons and sensors, and noticeably artificial in behavior and design, Android 16 most clearly looks and feels like an actual android.

Pokémon Trainers and Digimon Tamers

Is a Pokémon Trainer and feels like a Pokémon Trainer:
Ash Ketchum

Traveling from region to region, catching Pokémon, training a rotating team, battling Gym Leaders, entering leagues, and forming friendships with Pokémon make Ash the archetypal Pokémon Trainer.

Is a Pokémon Trainer but feels like a Digimon Tamer:
N

N's unusually personal connection with Pokémon, ability to understand them, discomfort with treating them as possessions, and emphasis on friendship over collecting give him a relationship with Pokémon that feels much closer to the bond between a Digimon Tamer and a partner Digimon.

Is a Digimon Tamer but feels like a Pokémon Trainer:
Rika Nonaka

Competitive, strategic, battle-focused, and initially concerned with becoming stronger and winning, Rika approaches Digimon combat more like a serious Pokémon Trainer than many other Digimon Tamers do.

Is a Digimon Tamer and feels like a Digimon Tamer:
Tai Kamiya

His close one-partner bond with Agumon, leadership of a group of chosen children, reliance on emotional growth to unlock evolution, and adventures in the Digital World embody the classic Digimon Tamer archetype.

H. G. Wells and Jules Verne

Is an H. G. Wells book and feels like an H. G. Wells book:
The Time Machine

Its speculative science, distant future, social commentary, and interest in humanity, class, and evolution make The Time Machine one of the clearest examples of H. G. Wells's style.

Is an H. G. Wells book but feels like a Jules Verne book:
The First Men in the Moon

Its journey to another world, unusual method of transportation, exploration of an unfamiliar environment, and adventure structure give it a strong Jules Verne-style voyage-of-discovery quality.

Is a Jules Verne book but feels like an H. G. Wells book:
The Begum's Fortune

Its rival technological societies, industrial militarism, destructive superweapons, and darker concerns about the effects of science on civilization give it an unusually Wells-like tone for a Jules Verne novel.

Is a Jules Verne book and feels like a Jules Verne book:
Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas

Exotic travel, technological wonder, scientific speculation, exploration, and the extraordinary Nautilus make Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas one of the clearest expressions of Jules Verne's adventure style.

Shrek

Poppy

Looney Tunes and Hanna-Barbera Characters

Is a Looney Tunes character and feels like a Looney Tunes character:
Bugs Bunny

Clever, theatrical, self-assured, wisecracking, and constantly turning conflicts into elaborate comedy routines, Bugs Bunny is one of the clearest expressions of the classic Looney Tunes personality.

Is a Looney Tunes character but feels like a Hanna-Barbera character:
Speedy Gonzales

His simple recurring premise, recognizable personality gimmick, fast-paced chase comedy, and straightforward conflicts give Speedy Gonzales a quality somewhat reminiscent of classic Hanna-Barbera cartoon characters.

Is a Hanna-Barbera character but feels like a Looney Tunes character:
Top Cat

Fast-talking, clever, scheming, confident, and usually able to manipulate situations through wit and personality, Top Cat has much of the verbal comedy and mischievous energy associated with Looney Tunes characters.

Is a Hanna-Barbera character and feels like a Hanna-Barbera character:
Yogi Bear

His memorable catchphrases, simple recurring premise, lovable troublemaking, comic interactions with a more sensible companion, and sitcom-like cartoon structure make Yogi Bear one of the clearest expressions of the classic Hanna-Barbera style.

Disney, Nickelodeon, and Warner Bros. Movies

Is a Disney movie and feels like a Disney movie:
Cinderella

Its fairy-tale setting, kindhearted heroine, magical transformation, animal sidekicks, memorable songs, elegant romance, and clear good-versus-evil structure make Cinderella one of the clearest examples of the classic Disney identity.

Is a Disney movie but feels like a Nickelodeon movie:
Chicken Little

Its frantic comedy, exaggerated character designs, pop-culture humor, awkward-kid protagonist, and energetic mid-2000s attitude give Chicken Little an unusually Nickelodeon-like personality for a Walt Disney Animation Studios film.

Is a Disney movie but feels like a Warner Bros. movie:
Treasure Planet

Its edgier science-fiction atmosphere, rebellious teenage protagonist, rougher character personalities, fast-paced action, darker visual tone, and mixture of humor with swashbuckling adventure give Treasure Planet a more Warner Bros.-like personality than most traditional Disney animated films, despite having some musical themes.

Is a Nickelodeon movie but feels like a Disney movie:
Charlotte's Web

Its heartfelt talking-animal story, storybook atmosphere, emotional themes, musical moments, and family-oriented sincerity give Charlotte's Web a distinctly Disney-like quality despite being a Nickelodeon Movies production.

Is a Nickelodeon movie and feels like a Nickelodeon movie:
The SpongeBob SquarePants Movie

Bright colors, surreal humor, childish enthusiasm, bizarre characters, slapstick, and an unapologetically weird sense of comedy make The SpongeBob SquarePants Movie one of the clearest expressions of Nickelodeon's identity.

Is a Nickelodeon movie but feels like a Warner Bros. movie:
Rango

Its eccentric Western setting, darker comedy, cinematic action, strange characters, elaborate visual style, and Looney Tunes-like cartoon energy give Rango a surprisingly Warner Bros.-like personality.

Is a Warner Bros. movie but feels like a Disney movie:
The Iron Giant

Its expressive traditional animation, heartfelt coming-of-age story, lovable nonhuman companion, emotional sincerity, and strong moral center give The Iron Giant many qualities associated with classic Disney animation.

Is a Warner Bros. movie but feels like a Nickelodeon movie:
Osmosis Jones

Its gross-out humor, bizarre body-themed world, exaggerated cartoon characters, kid-oriented irreverence, and mixture of comedy with strange biological concepts make Osmosis Jones feel unusually compatible with Nickelodeon's style.

Is a Warner Bros. movie and feels like a Warner Bros. movie:
Looney Tunes: Back in Action

Anarchic slapstick, fourth-wall-breaking humor, Hollywood satire, rapid-fire visual gags, classic Warner Bros. characters, and enormous cartoon spectacle make Looney Tunes: Back in Action quintessentially Warner Bros.
